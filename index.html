Research

I am currently working on probabilistic consensus sequence inference for viral genomes: given noisy sequencing reads from a diagnostic sample, how much can a model tell us about the underlying genome, and how confident should it be at each base? My approach builds a profile hidden Markov model that carries uncertainty all the way through, reporting a per-base posterior probability rather than a single hard call. It is benchmarked against the standard variant-calling stack (iVar, Medaka, BCFtools) across divergence categories, insertion and deletion scenarios, and sequencing-depth regimes, and it holds up best exactly where conventional pipelines degrade: low depth and high divergence.

I also work on Bayesian methods for clinical trials, where variational inference makes interim monitoring cheap enough to run often.
